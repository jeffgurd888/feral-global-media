# LANE 1 — THE ATOM: The Gravitational Atom, a Dossier
**Bridge artifact, 2026-10-24. Tier: T4 — sourced summary of the literature. NO CLAIM; nothing here is the program's result.**

## What it is
If a bosonic field with ultralight mass μ exists near a rotating (Kerr) black hole, waves satisfying the superradiance condition ω < mΩ_H are amplified, extracting the hole's rotational energy — up to ~10% of the hole's mass-energy can transfer into the field (literature, reviewed in the sources below). The bosons accumulate in bound states whose spectrum is hydrogenic: a gravitational fine-structure constant α = GMμ/(ħc) sets the levels, E_n ≈ −μc²α²/(2n²) at small α, with cloud radius r_c ~ GM/(c²α²). The literature's own name for hole + cloud is the **gravitational atom** — the analogy to hydrogen is mathematical (same Schrödinger-like bound-state problem), not decorative.

## What it does (the phenomenology)
- **Spin-down:** the cloud grows at the hole's expense until the superradiance condition saturates; holes that *should* have been spun down but are observed spinning fast exclude the corresponding boson masses. From stellar-mass black-hole spins, the currently excluded scalar-boson band is ~**[10⁻¹³, 10⁻¹²] eV** (neglecting non-gravitational interactions; search-verified 2026-10-24).
- **Annihilation radiation:** pairs of cloud bosons annihilate into gravitons, emitting nearly monochromatic gravitational waves at ω ≈ 2μ — a continuous-wave target for LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA all-sky searches (run; no detection to date, further disfavoring parts of the band).
- **Level transitions:** transitions between cloud states emit at Bohr-type frequencies ∝ μα²(n_g⁻² − n_e⁻²) — for stellar-mass holes this lands in the audio band; for supermassive holes, in LISA's. A literature even exists on *stimulated* transitions in these atoms driven by a stochastic GW background — the dropped document's "stimulated emission" phrase, existing for real, one lane over, in the one system where it belongs.
- **Occupation:** clouds are macroscopic quantum states in the plainest sense — occupation numbers estimated at N ~ 10⁷⁵–10⁹⁹ in the literature's worked ranges.
